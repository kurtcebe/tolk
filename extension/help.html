Tolk help

Tolk is a reader for the language you're learning. It reads the text aloud and highlights each word as it's spoken, so you can follow along at a speed that suits you. It translates what you select, and keeps the words you want to remember.

Tolk is still being built. For now, clicking its toolbar icon opens a sample text in Dutch.

Book and Learner

Both modes show the text in exactly the same place, so you can switch at any time, at the top right, without losing your line.

  • Learner keeps the player on screen, shows the tools beside each paragraph, and underlines your saved words.
  • Book hides all of that for quiet reading. The speaker button at the top right brings the player back, without starting to speak. Switching to Book pauses any speech. Selecting text still shows its translation.

Reading along

Press Play to start. A band marks the current sentence, and a stronger colour marks the current word.

  • Jump to a word: once reading has started, click any word to continue from there. This also works while paused.
  • Select text: dragging across text or double-clicking a word selects it, without moving your place.
  • Hear one thing: Hear in the popup reads only what you selected. Read this paragraph, beside a paragraph in Learner mode, reads that paragraph. Both stop by themselves, and clicking them again stops them early. If reading had already started, it waits, paused, at the place you'd reached.
  • Move by sentence: use the previous and next buttons.
  • Scrolling: the text holds still while the highlight moves down the page, then scrolls once to bring it back up. If you scroll yourself, the page stops following and the voice carries on. Choose Back to reading position to return.

Speed

The mark in the middle of the slider is the voice's normal speed. Tolk doesn't show percentages because each voice responds differently, so normal is the only speed it can promise. Reset takes you back there.

Some voices change speed in steps, so a small move may sound the same. How slow a voice can go also differs from one voice to the next, and trying another voice may get you a slower speed.

Voices

The speaker button in the player lists the voices for the language of the text. There are two kinds:

  • Local voices come with your computer and run on it. Tolk uses one by default.
  • Remote voices come with Chrome and send the text to Google to be spoken. Tolk only uses one if you pick it, and the player tells you while it's selected.

Your choice is remembered on this computer. If you use Chrome on another computer, you choose again there.

If there's no voice for your language

Tolk can't download voices itself. Add one in your computer's own settings, under speech, spoken content or language. The exact place depends on your system. You may need to restart Chrome before the new voice shows up in Tolk.

If only the sentence is highlighted

Not every voice reports which word it's on. Tolk finds this out when a voice first speaks, and then highlights the sentence alone rather than guess a word. A remote voice usually works this way. A local voice usually highlights each word, so try one if you want word highlighting.

Translating

Translation runs on your computer, using a language pack that Chrome downloads once. Selecting text never starts that download, but translating a paragraph does, because clicking it is a clear request.

  • A word or phrase: select it, and a popup shows the translation. A partly selected word counts as the whole word. Click elsewhere or press Esc to close the popup.
  • A paragraph: in Learner mode, hover over a paragraph or reach it with Tab, and choose the Translate button in the margin. A translated line appears under each sentence. Choose it again to remove them. In a narrow window, the tools are behind a ⋯ button above the paragraph.

A word on its own has no sentence around it, so its translation can be the wrong sense of the word. If it seems odd, select the whole sentence. Translations can be wrong in other ways too, so treat them as help rather than as an authority.

Saved words

Press the star in the popup to save a word or phrase of up to 20 words, along with its translation and the sentence it came from. Press it again to remove it. If translation isn't available, the word is saved without one.

The star at the top right opens your list. Each word is listed once per language, with the sentence you first saved it from. In Learner mode, saved words have a dotted underline wherever they appear again. Only the same whole word counts, so other forms of a word aren't underlined.

Using them in Anki

  1. Choose Export for Anki (TSV) in the list. If you've saved words in several languages, pick a language first, so each can go in its own deck.
  2. In Anki, choose File and then Import, and pick the file.
  3. Match the Word, Translation, Sentence and Source columns to your note type's fields, and import.

This needs Anki 2.1.54 or later.

Languages

Tolk works with two languages: the one you're reading, and your own. The text stays readable whatever Tolk knows about either. Both are in the settings, under the Aa button.

  • Your own language is what Tolk will translate into. Tolk asks for it once, the first time it opens, and you can change it in the settings.
  • The language of the text is detected from the text itself. If Tolk gets it wrong, choose Change and pick another. That lasts until you close or reload the page, and Use detected language undoes it.

If Tolk can't tell, the language shows as Unknown. Choose one to start reading aloud. If Chrome only needs a download to detect languages, the settings offer it.

Translation status

The settings show whether Chrome can translate from the language of the text into yours on this device.

  • Ready: it's on this device.
  • Needs download: choose Download and Chrome fetches it. Nothing downloads until you click. This can show even when Chrome already has the language, and the download then finishes straight away.
  • Not available: this device or language pair isn't supported. chrome://on-device-internals shows what your device can do.

Keyboard shortcuts

These work while the player is visible, except when you're in a control that uses the same key, such as the speed slider. An open panel also closes when you click the text beside it.

Key What it does
Space Play or pause
← → Previous or next sentence
[ ] Slower or faster
Esc Close the popup, then the voice list or the open panel

Privacy

  • Tolk has no account, analytics or crash reports.
  • Tolk doesn't send your reading anywhere itself.
  • The one exception is a remote voice, if you pick one: Chrome sends the text to Google to be spoken.
  • Your settings, such as colours, speed and your own language, follow your Chrome profile, so Chrome stores them in your Google account. Your voice choice stays on this computer.
  • If you choose a download, Chrome fetches that language model from Google. Your reading isn't sent with it, and detecting a language and translating both happen on this device.
  • Saved words stay in this browser and never go to your Google account. Uninstalling Tolk deletes them, so export any you want to keep.
